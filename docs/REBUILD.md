# Quarantine rebuild — design and validation

## Direction

Stylized 2.5D survival action: orthographic perspective, planar movement, sculpted French buildings and animated characters. Cold dusk, amber lamps, subdued materials, visible combat feedback and an unobstructed aiming area. Coal, concrete, bone and emergency orange; condensed display typography with small operational labels. The rendered world doubles as title artwork. Graphics are procedural low-poly assets, not a claim of AAA studio asset fidelity.

## Implementation

Eight generated biomes and 32 waves, with a boss every fourth wave and final extraction. Seeded layouts vary from 52–72 units wide and 44–58 deep, using four outlines: broken block, crossroads, highland basin and river bend. Jungle, mountains, badlands and marsh extend the original city, docks, gardens and foundry. Each district has its own boss name and appearance palette; the eight encounters reuse four attack archetypes.

Layout generation reserves arrival, boss and extraction space; validates the playable polygon and cover connectivity at boss radius; and derives safe spawn points from the actual boundary. Movement, dashes, direct routes and pathfinding respect concave edges. Buildings follow boundary segments. Each district derives its own seed independently of combat randomness.

Version 4.2 allows two firearms or one exclusive melee weapon. Guns target, fire and reload independently. Weapon pairs unlock plasma arc, shattershot, double tap and breach & clear, while matching guns use staggered akimbo fire; perks add thermal shock, deadshot, second wind and demolitionist. Four permanent upgrade tracks supplement run-specific perks.

Breakable objects include cars, barriers, planted trees, crates, barrels, rocks, urban facades and individual industrial buildings. Buildings have 2,400 health and three degradation stages at 75%, 50% and 25% remaining health. Impacts leave soot/crack decals and shatter panes; stage transitions strip wall sections and throw masonry before final collapse. Blast damage uses the nearest surface rather than the building center. Small set dressing, wilderness perimeter trees/mountains and the ground/boundary remain scenery. This is staged visual destruction, not a rigid-body fracture simulation.

Ballistic headshots are chance-based (32% rifle, 22% revolver, 7% other ballistic weapons) with extra damage and head fragments. Surviving heavy hits may sever an arm or legs; crawlers continue attacking at reduced speed and damage. Death visuals distinguish ordinary shots, exploding heads, blast dismemberment, burns and electrocution. Gore settings suppress fragments and blood. Flames use animated fractal noise, turbulence, changing opacity and rising embers; explosion debris varies in scale, shape, spin, velocity and bounce.

## 4.1 open city, vehicles and material effects

Default Open City uses a 232×232 connected grid, 22 destructible buildings, four canal crossings and seeded building heights, dimensions, materials, trees, cover and parked vehicles. Eight landmarks reward exploration; their names reference campaign locations, but the city itself renders urban, park and industrial areas. The original eight-biome campaign remains selectable. City geometry and damage persist through the 32-wave progression; distant ordinary enemies relocate to the vicinity of the player. The atlas pauses action and supports street/landmark waypoints; its line is a destination bearing, not turn-by-turn routing.

V enters or exits nearby cars. Directional steering has acceleration, angular smoothing, coasting and Space braking. Vehicle hull absorbs attacks, impacts damage cars, speed determines ramming damage, and entering the canal sinks the vehicle and ejects the survivor onto the last safe ground. Vehicles share a base car body and arcade handling; there is no traffic AI or general rigid-body physics.

Katana and dual swords extend the arsenal to ten weapons and add Blade Dance. Both can be equipped alongside guns. Three-hit sequences alternate swings and widen the finisher; blades consume no ammunition, cut limbs and damage timber more strongly than masonry. Animation now uses continuously interpolated body, knee and elbow motion, with idle breathing, movement blending and sword twists. Scout, Medic, Rider and Ronin have distinct palettes and headgear.

Deaths select among six fall poses with randomized speed and twist, combined with existing headshot, explosion, burn, electricity and crawling outcomes. Brick, concrete, wood, metal, glass and earth debris use separate profiles for geometry, fragment size, surface, spin and bounce. Falling particles detect water and trigger appropriately sized splashes. Small splashes are grouped spatially to avoid a pile of bright rings. Water explosions suppress flames and use pale spray/smoke; dock and canal surfaces use their correct elevations.

Buildings shed roughly 24%, 48% and 72% of exterior sections across their three damage stages. Final destruction sinks and tilts the structure, removes collision and leaves scattered rubble. Facade panels and windows are combined into material batches for rendering, then rebuilt after damage. Source sections retain their damage state, so batching does not restore broken walls. Foreground buildings fade only when they intersect the camera-to-survivor line. The city overview uses a more distant camera to avoid clipping.

Shader compilation occurs before the menu opens. No remote assets or fonts are required. GitHub Actions builds and deploys the tested production artifact with relative asset paths.

## 4.3 inhabited streets and encounter variety

Design intent: preserve the coal/bone/amber field interface and condensed display type while making the world more recognisable at gameplay distance. Priorities were repeated silhouettes, flat street materials, identical enemies and buildings masking the hero. Use geometry and material detail tied to the setting, rather than full-screen decorative effects. UI additions use an 8/12/16 px rhythm, square edges, operational mono labels and a small left-side radio readout; they do not compete with aiming or wave information.

City buildings now derive one of five architectural styles from their block: tenement, hotel, civic, foundry or warehouse. Rooflines, fixtures, warm windows, shutters, balconies, striped awnings, loading doors, industrial metal panels/pipes, posters and addresses differentiate them. Streets add paving, drainage, crossings, signage and irregular wet patches. Parks have textured lawns, paths and one of three central furniture arrangements. Decorative street furniture remains scenery; existing cover and buildings retain their collision/destruction rules.

Occlusion tests use parallel orthographic sightlines and a shoulder margin down to the feet. Obstructing buildings clip to 0.7 m walls with a translucent footprint instead of stacking transparent floors over the player. Roof decorations are grouped with their parent roof, so destroyed roofs do not leave floating dormers. Five facade styles still use the staged structural destruction system.

Six civilian variants change clothing, headgear and facial details without consuming simulation randomness. Rounded torsos and tapered arms reduce the block shape; clothing and face details are batched separately from animated joints. Kill events carry appearance identity into corpses.

Three city side encounters are placed at reachable, separated street points using the run seed. Each has a distinct guard arrangement and reward. The relay requires eight uncontested seconds nearby on foot after its guards die; the convoy and enforcer require clearing their marked guards. Optional guards count towards the current wave and do not disappear when the player leaves. Completion is permanent for the run. Existing 32-wave progression and eight-biome campaign remain intact.

## 4.5 weapon motion and combat timing

The old torso-mounted generic gun and disconnected arm rotations are replaced by distinct weapon rigs and two-bone arm solving. The main hand holds the trigger grip; a free hand follows the foregrip, reaches down during reloads and returns to support. Paired weapons have separate models, recoil clocks, magazines and moving actions. Rifles, SMGs, revolvers, shotgun pumps, a marksman bolt/scope, fuel tanks, launcher barrels and arc coils have different silhouettes. Barrel-mounted flashes and spent casings reinforce shots. Heavy weapons settle more slowly and sway less than light weapons.

Player footsteps advance with actual travel rather than elapsed time. Strafing/backpedalling use movement relative to aim, and movement blends down when blocked. Pausing freezes the player's gait and blade trails. Blade key poses return smoothly to guard rather than resetting a full-body spin. The katana has two-handed cuts and a heavier third stroke; twin swords alternate lead hands before a paired finisher. Narrow, fading ribbons sample the actual blades.

Melee simulation and rendering share attack duration/contact timing. Damage, impact sound and vibration occur at contact, once per stroke, after anticipation. A late manual press queues one follow-up; holding attack continues the sequence. Dodging, switching, entering a car or throwing a grenade cancels pending blade damage. Throw recovery prevents firing a hidden firearm. The HUD distinguishes wind-up, slash, recovery and the tap-to-chain window. Existing controller, keyboard and touch inputs feed this shared combat path.

## 4.8 weapon evolution and surface gore

Design intent: keep the coal/bone/orange field-equipment interface, Impact headings, mono readouts, square borders and compact spacing. Weapon silhouettes, material response, mechanical motion and actual model portraits carry the visual distinction. A thin mastery meter supplements the existing ammunition display; controller labels and touch controls retain their positions.

All ten weapons have four hardware/finish appearances, four procedural shot or cut variations, specific reload motion and independently animated mechanical parts. Run-local mastery uses actual enemy health removed, with thresholds at 1,000/3,500/8,000 damage. Empty shots and scenery do not farm mastery; overkill is excluded. Identical guns share mastery but retain independent magazines. Field Armorer grants 650 mastery per distinct equipped type. The final tier adds named weapon-specific mechanics including finite launcher subcharges, revolver fourth-shot explosions, rifle terminal bursts, electrical cascades, fire pools and blade finisher waves.

Nearest-contact bullet ordering fixes cover/enemy ordering and thin-cover muzzle tunnelling. Penetration attenuates damage, grazing hard surfaces can reflect evolved SMG rounds, launcher rounds have gravity, and knockback respects enemy mass. Fragment drag, restitution, friction and water contact are shared. Muzzle flashes use soft directional shaders; flame jets follow the barrel, casings eject at mechanism-appropriate times, and impacts distinguish materials. Particle budgets remain bounded.

Blood droplets follow hit direction and gravity, intersect actual scenery triangles, and project clipped stains onto walls, intact windows, props, vehicles and pavement. Missing panels do not catch splashes. Stains attach to their hit mesh, follow its movement, respect building cutaways and disappear with replaced/destroyed surfaces. Sixteen procedural texture variants use rounded irregular pools, satellite droplets and vertical runs. Fresh stains darken and become rougher over time. Blood does not pool on water; heat damage does not produce ballistic spray. Nearby stains coalesce spatially, airborne blood is capped at 90, stains at 150 and age at three minutes. The gore toggle clears existing blood, gibs and corpses. Surface stains are projected textures with simulated droplets, not a fluid solver.

## 4.10 combat feedback and critical progression

Design intent: preserve coal/bone field equipment, condensed Impact damage digits, small mono wound labels and hard dark outlines. Semantic colors distinguish bullets, burn, shock, blast and slice; critical hits add gold brackets and a short scale impulse. Larger values earn larger type through a bounded logarithmic scale. Recently hit enemy bars show immediate health loss plus a short trailing chip. No new persistent HUD panel competes with the weapon or survival controls.

Critical resolution now lives at the enemy damage boundary. Each direct weapon hit rolls its weapon's base chance plus Deadeye and evolution bonuses, capped at 80%, for exactly double damage. This covers firearms, blade contacts, arc chains and weapon explosions. Headshots retain their separate 1.65 multiplier. Periodic burn damage and executions never roll criticals; environmental damage has no weapon critical chance. The original Deadeye-plus-piercing execution requirement remains bonus-based, so base critical chances do not unlock it for everyone. Invalid damage is ignored. Feedback receives exact resolved hit strength, actual health removed, health/max health, wound type, tier and critical status. Overkill can appear in the number, but mastery still awards only actual health removed.

A separate 2D canvas projects into the same camera without adding WebGL draw calls or intercepting input. It merges matching 120 ms bursts (420 ms for burn ticks), expires independently of aggregation, freezes with paused gameplay, caps stored numbers at 48 and considers at most 32 visible health bars. It prioritizes critical/heavy hits and rejects overlapping labels when the nearby screen area is full. A first crowded-scene review exposed a tall number column; bounded nearby placement and bar overlap rejection replaced it before release. ## 4.13 structural collapse and living environments

Building durability now scales with volume. Destruction replaces the sinking whole-building animation with seeded wall/floor bodies, staggered floor release, gravity at 9.81 m/s², small collision substeps, damping, angular motion, secondary fractures and sleeping rubble. Broad-phase filtering reduces collision work. Bodies hit neighbouring walls, rest on roofs, fall again when that support disappears, and damage nearby actors on hard ground impacts. Settled piles become destructible cover; destroying cover clears its matching visible fragments. Masonry and metal retain distinct roughness/colors, with aggregate texture and irregular geometry. Impact dust follows collisions rather than one fixed explosion burst.

This is a bounded gameplay approximation: coarse body contacts and grid pile support, rather than pairwise convex rigid-body contacts, stress/beam engineering or fluid dynamics. The shared structural budget retains up to 1,200 bodies; old sleeping fragments retire first around a 720-body target, with matching cover removed to avoid invisible collisions. Cosmetic particle budget remains 420 and does not determine fire, damage or extinguishing. Multiple distant demolitions can retire older rubble. A single staged six-storey tower generated 192 primary bodies and 310 after impact fragmentation, all asleep by eight seconds, with 52 cover piles.

Combustible window interiors/facade bays have heat, finite fuel, wetness and scorch state. Heat spreads sideways and upward, and ground-level burning bays can ignite nearby foliage and enemies. Concrete/rock/metal rubble is not fuel. Flame and burning-enemy contact use the same ignition path. Water shots, water explosions and structural water contacts extinguish within a bounded radius; wetted actors, bays and ground resist immediate reignition. Steam appears when water quenches active fire. Fountain spray maintains wet basin areas. The model operates on surface cells and radial splash volumes, not individual visual droplets.

City seeds now place park ponds and fountains, with curved boundaries shared by water detection, rendering and atlas. Navigation uses conservative narrow strips across ponds. Park paving is cut around water instead of covering it. Added reeds, lily pads, stone edges, grass and up to 100 destructible trees; vacant-ground growth preserves road corridors and safe spawn points. Canopy shaders add gentle motion. Fountains have six arcing jets and surface ripples.
